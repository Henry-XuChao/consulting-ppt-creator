# Independent consulting presentation system

Use this system only when the user has not supplied a template or approved brand package. User-supplied brand guidance always overrides it.

## Brand posture

- Aim for professional-services credibility: analytical, understated, direct, and human.
- Use green as a precise signal, not as a decorative wash.
- Keep the canvas spacious and the hierarchy obvious. Favor one clear idea over dense component grids.
- Describe the result as an independent consulting style, not as an official third-party template.

## Color system

Use these core colors:

| Role | Color |
| --- | --- |
| Primary background | White `#FFFFFF` |
| Primary text / dark field | Black `#000000` |
| Signature accent | Green `#86BC25` |
| Dark gray | `#53565A` |
| Mid gray | `#A7A8AA` |
| Light gray | `#D0D0CE` |

Use the accent green for key numerals, a short rule, a dot, a chart highlight, or the single recommended option. Keep most slides neutral. For data requiring additional series, add colors sparingly and consistently, preferring `#43B02A`, `#046A38`, `#0076A8`, and gray before introducing unrelated hues. Reserve red, orange, and yellow for functional warning or status meaning.

## Typography

- Use a clean sans serif available in the environment. Prefer Aptos, Arial, or Helvetica-compatible fallbacks unless an approved font is supplied.
- Do not claim that a fallback is a third party’s proprietary typeface.
- Use at least 50 pt for deck titles, 35 pt for slide titles, 24 pt for callout headers, and 16 pt for body copy when no template establishes another scale.
- Use bold weight and size—not many colors—to create hierarchy.
- Keep one-line titles on one line. Shorten copy or change layout before shrinking text.

## Layout language

- Use strong left alignment, generous margins, disciplined grids, and large areas of white or black.
- Favor asymmetric editorial compositions: a strong headline paired with one chart, image, number, or concise evidence block.
- Use thin rules, a small green dot, or a narrow green edge as recurring brand cues.
- Vary slide silhouettes across the deck while preserving consistent title, footer, and page-number positions.
- Avoid excessive rounded cards, gradients, shadows, glossy effects, dense dashboards, decorative icons, and green on every object.

## Recommended slide archetypes

### Cover

Use a minimal title, optional subtitle/date, and one restrained green accent. Choose either a white field with black type or a black field with white type. Do not add an improvised third-party wordmark.

### Executive message

Lead with one decisive takeaway and support it with three concise implications or evidence points. Avoid a generic agenda unless navigation is genuinely useful.

### Evidence or chart

Make the claim the title. Use neutral series and highlight only the decision-relevant series in green. Label values directly when feasible and state the implication near the chart.

### Comparison or options

Use a clean table or aligned columns. Highlight the recommended option with a green rule or marker rather than a filled green card.

### Process or roadmap

Use a simple left-to-right or top-to-bottom flow with no more stages than the story requires. Keep connectors behind nodes and use green for the current or priority stage.

### Section divider

Use a black field, a short section statement, and a single green accent. Keep it sparse.

### Recommendation and action

State the recommendation first, then show owners, timing, dependencies, and the immediate next decision. End by resolving the deck’s opening question.

## Imagery and logo handling

- Use imagery with editorial realism, purposeful cropping, and room for text. Avoid generic handshake, puzzle-piece, or glowing-technology stock clichés.
- Do not retrieve, redraw, recolor, stretch, or approximate a third-party logo unless the user supplies an approved asset or explicitly authorizes its use.
- When an approved logo is supplied, preserve its proportions and clear space. Do not use typed text or decorative shapes to imitate a wordmark.

## Voice

Write in a confident, clear, and human manner. Prefer concrete statements, plain language, and evidence-backed recommendations. Avoid inflated slogans, consultant filler, and unsupported certainty.

## Design basis

This fallback system is an original, independent set of guidance for clear and restrained business presentations. It does not claim affiliation with, or reproduce assets from, any third-party brand.
